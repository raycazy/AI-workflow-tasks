My 1st YearsOn-site searchA/B39,030 visitors

A search bar hiding behind an icon was costing a quarter of search revenue.

Observation

Only 8.5% of users touched on-site search, yet those who did converted at 12.5% against 5.6% without it. Roughly 2.25x the rate, locked behind a small magnifier icon.

Hypothesis

If we make search visible instead of hidden, more shoppers will use it, and because search users already convert far better, revenue will follow.

The change

We replaced the icon with a visible search bar on desktop and mobile, with microcopy inside it. Nothing else on the page moved.

Result

Search engagement rose +25.31% at 100% confidence, and search-purchase rose +26.10% at 97.46%. Statistically significant on both goals.

PH ImportAll PDPsA/B/CMay 2026

The space above add-to-cart was doing nothing. So we gave it a job.

Observation

The recommended product above ATC earned about 1% click-through on desktop and 0.7% on mobile, against 7% for the button itself. The upsell pulled 0.1%.

Hypothesis

If reassurance sits at the point of decision, it reinforces both trust and the bundle choice, lifting add-to-cart, conversion and revenue per visitor.

The change

A strip of assurance icons (free shipping over 300 kr, 100,000+ customers, 1 to 3 day delivery) placed directly below the bundle selector.

Result

Revenue per visitor rose +4.14%, worth $122,658 a month. Shipped to every PDP.

QookwareAll PDPsA/BMobile

87% of traffic was mobile. The buy button kept scrolling away.

Observation

Mobile drove 87% of sessions (188,631) but converted at only 4.9%, against 7.2% on desktop. Average PDP session: 74 seconds.

Hypothesis

If a sticky add-to-cart bar stays in view as shoppers scroll, fewer will drop off without adding, lifting mobile conversion and revenue per visitor.

The change

A fixed bottom bar that appears once the shopper scrolls past the native button, so the product can be added from anywhere on the page.

Result

Revenue per visitor rose +21.12% at 92% confidence on RPV and 99% on conversion rate. Shipped to the mobile segment.

ComforthPDPA/BApril 2026

Most shoppers never scrolled far enough to learn what it does.

Observation

Average scroll depth on the PDP was 35%. The shoppers who did read the description converted at a higher rate, so the key attributes were reaching too few people.

Hypothesis

If product tags sit above the fold, order value, conversion and revenue per visitor will rise, because attributes reach more shoppers at the decision moment.

The change

Product tags surfaced in the first fold of the PDP. The control had none.

Result

Revenue per visitor rose +10.83%, worth $26,596 a month.

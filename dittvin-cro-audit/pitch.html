Executive Summary
Six Findings That Move Revenue
Delivery is the number one trust wound. The site promises "1-3 dagar" but 31% of low star reviews report 7 to 16 days. 86% of complaints are about delivery, and loyal repeat buyers are churning on it.
Fix the promise, not the wine. This is a retention leak, not just acquisition.
The legality question is unanswered. In a monopoly market, a first time Swedish buyer needs to know this is legal. dittvin explains nothing, while KRAN and Winefamly reassure directly. Fear tips to "Tror detta r bedrägeri".
Most buyers are first time visitors, and they need this reassurance.
Taste is invisible online. The biggest barrier in online wine is not knowing how it tastes. dittvin's boxes list region and grape but rarely translate them into sweetness, body, and food pairing the way a shop assistant would, so buyers hesitate.
Make taste legible on every box: flavor profiles, pairings, and Vivino scores.
Checkout abandonment is the biggest leak. 58.9% of sessions that reach checkout do not complete (17,785 of 30,200). Delivery doubt, the "1-3 dagar" promise, and 90+ website errors are prime suspects.
Highest intent, cheapest to recover. Est. 3 to 5M SEK/quarter.
Retention is a strength to scale. 66% of buyers are returning customers, and dittvin already runs a 3 tier subscription (Wine, Gin, Champagne, 749 to 849 SEK). The engine exists.
Grow and showcase the subscription. Make it more prominent and add it to more product pages.
GA4 is not tracking properly. It records only 31% of orders (11,689 vs 38,306) and inflates sessions with junk. A Google Ads conversion also fires on the homepage. Decisions must run on Shopify until it is rebuilt.
Fix measurement with server side tracking (Meta) so the ad platforms bid on full signal.

The Number One Buying Hesitation
You Cannot Taste It Before You Buy
Taste is the biggest hesitation in online wine. Everyone likes something different (dry or sweet, light or full, fruity or bitter) and nobody can try before they buy. When the flavor is a gamble, people hesitate, fall back on price, and often pick a wine they do not enjoy, then blame the shop. Show the taste up front and each buyer chooses a wine they will actually love.
The problem, in your own reviews

Buying blind leads to the wrong bottle

14% of complaints are about taste. A box of 12 unknown wines is a leap of faith, so price and discount become the only things to go on. When the wine does not match what someone expected, they feel let down even when it is a good wine.

"Vinerna smakar som saft" (the wines taste like juice)

This buyer wanted something drier. With a simple taste profile they would have picked a different wine and stayed happy.

"Jag saknar en beskrivning av respektive vin och vad det passar till." (I miss a description of each wine and what it pairs with)
The fix: a taste profile on every wine and box

Show the flavor before the buy

Each buyer self selects the style they like, which lifts add to cart, powers the box ladder, and cuts both the "smakar som saft" complaints and the returns they cause.

The Trust Equation
In Swedish online alcohol, two levers decide the first purchase: is it legal, and will it arrive. dittvin is silent on the first and unreliable on the second.
Market Context · Highest Leverage Gap
Nobody Told the Swede It Is Legal
Sweden runs the Systembolaget monopoly. dittvin lawfully ships as a Danish cross border private importer (Vinhuset ApS). It never explains this, so doubt fills the gap.
What dittvin does today

Silence on legality

Zero mention of Systembolaget, private import, Swedish alcohol tax, or that VAT is included. The Danish operator identity is buried in the footer. The genuine assets (a 4.7 Trustpilot, award medals, Løvens Hule TV backing) sit far from the point of doubt.

"Så in i norden dåligt. leveransen har inte kommit fast det har gått 2 veckor. Tror detta r bedrägeri. VARNING" (I think this is fraud. WARNING)
What the best rival does

KRAN states it plainly

"Vi lyder under den svenska alkohollagstiftningen och regleras av svenska Skatteverket som distansförsäljare." Winefamly reassures with "Alla priser inkl. svensk alkoholskatt & moms." Winefinder, Winefamly and Supervin also show the Trygg E-handel trust mark. dittvin shows none of it.

The fix: a plain "Is this legal? Yes, and here is how" block on the product page and cart, with the age check, the Danish import model, and tax included stated up front.

Two Walls Before a Single Product
Cold Facebook visitors (41% of traffic) land and hit a legally required age check stacked on top of a spin the wheel email popup, before they see any wine.
Live mobile entry · captured 2026-07-23
Two popups in a row. The age check ("bekräfta att du är 20 år eller äldre") is legally needed, but a "Snurra på hjulet" spin the wheel popup stacks on top of it. People have to close two popups before they see a single wine.
Combine them into one step, and remember the choice with a cookie so repeat visitors are not asked again.
Turn the age gate into a trust moment. Right now it only blocks. Add one line: lawful Danish import, tax included. It is the perfect place to answer the legality question.
Emoji heavy promo bar. The top bar (party and clock emoji) clashes with a "kvalitetsviner" premium promise on a wine brand.
Chat and Klaviyo present. Support and capture tools are live. The issue is stacking and sequence, not absence.

“Paper straws and cups leave microplastic and residues which cause cancer.”

Straw

Residues partly supported; cancer link not supported

Residues are real: a Belgian study found PFAS (“forever chemicals” often found in paper straw coatings) in 18 of 20 paper straw brands, and a lined paper cup released about 25,000 microplastic particles into hot water in a lab. But the same study found PFAS in 4 of 5 bamboo straw brands, a US study found PFAS in plant based straws too, and the authors call the risk from occasional use limited. IARC classes PFOA as Group 1, carcinogenic to humans, but says this does not indicate the level of risk from a given exposure. No study linking straw or cup use to cancer was found.

So a jute straw cannot claim to be PFAS free until its own lab test says so.

Sources: Groffen et al. 2023; Timshina et al. 2021; IARC; WHO

“A loofah should not be used for more than a week, because it builds up bacteria and fungus.”

Loofah

Partly true: risk real, interval wrong

Loofahs do grow bacteria once wet, and one Pseudomonas skin infection was traced to a loofah. The guidance is to replace a natural loofah every 3 to 4 weeks and a plastic one after about 2 months, cleaning it weekly and replacing it at once if it smells or shows mould. Natural loofahs get the shorter life, which supports a refill pack, not a weekly fear message.

Sources: Cleveland Clinic; Bottone et al. 1994

“It will decompose in a few days in a flower pot or in the ground.”

Loofah and straw

Not supported

No soil burial study of a luffa sponge or jute stick was found. Woody materials can take up to two years to break down unless shredded; the home compost certificate allows 12 months for 90% breakdown. The only jute straw figure on record is the maker's own “5 years” in open air. Leave any time claim off the pack until your own test sets it.

Sources: UMaine Extension; TÜV OK compost HOME; jute straw paper, 2022

“Paper making is water, tree and resource intensive.”

Also in the brief

Partly true: energy supported; water and trees partly

European mills used about 13.3 GJ of energy a tonne in 2024 and drew 3,160 million m³ of water, but returned about 90% of it after treatment, and recovered paper equals 58.5% of their output. The tree point applies to virgin pulp, not to all paper. A comparison claim on a pack needs like for like evidence.

Source: Cepi Key Statistics 2025

A jute stick straw is feasible in principle and already hand made, but nothing about it is proven for cafes or export

Verdict: Feasible, not yet proven. It is a single natural stem with no glue, like wheat, reed and grass straws. Five risks decide it: an open bore, uneven diameter with no wide sizes, a steady supply of the “special quality” stick, microbes from 20 days of retting in pond or river water, and splinters.

What is known

  • Jute Stick Straw, Krishnanagar, Nadia, West Bengal: 11 to 25 staff, 6 years on IndiaMART, free samples; trade price ₹2.50 to 3 a straw at a 2,000 minimum order, from a search snippet unverified (company profile, no product or price shown)
  • Go Green Bangladesh, Jashore: factory since 2020, capacity over 100,000 straws a day with about 5% in use, a warning sign on demand; 15 women earn at least Tk 200 a day
  • Size: 20 cm long, 2.5 to 5 mm across, about 1.4 g; no 8 to 12 mm size for smoothies or boba
  • Process as the maker describes it: about 20 days of water retting, fibre stripped, 10 to 12 days of sun drying, cut, bore cleaned, boiled 10 minutes with neem leaves or salt
  • The Krishnanagar maker's blog reports “problems after problems” with hole size; Go Green says sourcing the right stick was its main difficulty
  • No US or EU shop sells a jute stick straw, and nobody sells a branded, certified one
  • Wheat stem is the benchmark: HAY! lists BPI, USDA BioPreferred and an EU 10/2011 migration test

What must be tested before any branding money

  1. Bore: share of sticks open end to end, inner diameter, usable straws per kg
  2. Wet strength at 30 minutes, 1 hour and 3 hours in cold water, lemonade, milk shake and 85 °C coffee
  3. Taste and smell panel against wheat stem and paper
  4. Microbiology after boiling: plate count, yeast and mould, coliforms
  5. Food contact migration (EU 10/2011 or FSSAI), heavy metals, pesticide residue and a PFAS screen
  6. Splinters and mouth feel after cutting and sanding
  7. Home compost time, ideally an OK compost HOME test
  8. Mould in humid storage over 6 to 12 months

A precedent for the two product idea: the Krishnanagar jute straw maker says it already added luffa.

we have added another new product called luffa sponges

It lists leaf and nut plates, jute handicrafts and organic garments as next. The same source reports “problems after problems” with hole size and says it moved to a straw combining bamboo and jute. Source: the maker's blog, repeated word for word in Rahman, Sultana and Jahangir, 2022, which reports no tests.

Whether the stick is naturally hollow end to end rests on the maker's word only; a plant anatomy paper describes it as woody tissue with no named cavity, and that paper was later retracted. Treat hollowness as a claim to test.

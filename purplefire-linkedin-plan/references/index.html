Before/after creative: the references

What the redesign of our result cards is based on: public sources that publish real A/B before/after visuals, what each one does well, and where to browse the LinkedIn native examples that sit behind a login.

1 · GoodUI Leaks: the closest analog to our format

Source: goodui.org/leaks · Jakub Linowski publishes A/B screenshots leaked from big ecommerce sites

Why it matters: the industry's reference format for before/after test visuals. Two screenshots side by side, labeled A and B with dates, and the implemented winner marked with a colored border. No captions, no arrows, no explanation on the image itself. The screenshots are tightly cropped to the tested element (Etsy's add to cart button, Walmart's product layout).

Stolen for our cards: the green border marking the winner, A/B tags directly on the screenshots, and crops so tight the difference is findable in about a second.

2 · Marketing Examples: one idea per visual

Source: marketingexamples.com · Harry Dry's gallery, the benchmark for minimal marketing visuals

Why it matters: every tile carries exactly one idea in one line of large type over one image ("This rebrand cost Tropicana $55M", with just two juice cartons). No stat blocks, no logos rows, no footers. Stolen for our cards: a single number with a single label as the entire text layer, and the discipline to move everything else into the post copy.

3 · GoodUI Patterns: proof presented as a system

Source: goodui.org/patterns

Why it matters: GoodUI's credibility comes from publishing losers alongside winners (43 losing, 165 winning on the front page). It's the same honesty posture as our "trending, not proven" chips and the flat test post in week 7.

5 · Format research (text sources)

  • AuthoredUp: best performing LinkedIn content 2026: one idea per frame, minimal text per visual
  • Marin Istvanic on creative concepts: split screen comparisons work because they're instantly parseable and carry a clear hypothesis
  • Postiv: LinkedIn post examples that drive results

What our v2 cards took from each

  • From GoodUI Leaks: A/B tags on the screenshots, green marks the winner, crops tight enough that the difference is the first thing you see.
  • From Marketing Examples: one number, one label, nothing else in the text layer. Significance and revenue figures live in the post copy.
  • From GoodUI Patterns: the honesty posture. Real screenshots from the live store, and the "before reconstructed" disclosure instead of a faked old page.
  • Our additions: the green highlight ring on the changed element (faster than a caption), the store URL for identity, and the PurpleFire logo for attribution. That's the whole card.
Honest limitation: image search engines (Google, DuckDuckGo, Bing) all captcha automated capture, and LinkedIn posts sit behind the auth wall, so the gallery above uses the public analogs instead of actual LinkedIn screenshots.

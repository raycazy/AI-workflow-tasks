Executive Summary
Six Findings That Move Revenue
The store is bigger and healthier than the dashboard shows. Shopify: 33.3M SEK gross (41.5M incl VAT), 38,306 orders in 90 days (25 April to 23 July 2026) at a 3.69% conversion rate. Wine boxes convert far above single bottles.
This audit is built on Shopify, the source of truth. GA4 records only ~29%.
Delivery is the number one trust wound. The site promises "1-3 dagar" but 31% of low star reviews report 7 to 16 days. 86% of complaints are about delivery, and loyal repeat buyers are churning on it.
Fix the promise, not the wine. This is a retention leak, not just acquisition.
The legality question is unanswered. In a monopoly market, a first time Swedish buyer needs to know this is legal. dittvin explains nothing, while KRAN and Winefamly reassure directly. Fear tips to "Tror detta r bedrägeri".
Most buyers are first time visitors, and they need this reassurance.
Checkout abandonment is the biggest leak. 58.9% of sessions that reach checkout do not complete (17,785 of 30,200). Delivery doubt, the "1-3 dagar" promise, and 90+ JS console errors are prime suspects.
Highest intent, cheapest to recover. Est. 3 to 5M SEK/quarter.
Retention is a strength to scale, not a gap. 66% of buyers are returning customers, and dittvin already runs a 3 tier subscription (Wine, Gin, Champagne, 749 to 849 SEK). The engine exists.
Grow and merchandise the subscription. Do not launch one, it is already here.
GA4 is not tracking properly. It records only 31% of orders (11,689 vs 38,306) and inflates sessions with junk. A Google Ads conversion also fires on the homepage. Decisions must run on Shopify until it is rebuilt.
Fix measurement (server side + Meta CAPI) so the ad platforms bid on full signal.

The Category's Biggest Barrier
You Cannot Taste It Before You Buy
Online wine has one structural disadvantage a shop does not: no tasting. When flavor is a gamble, buyers hesitate, default to price, and blame the wine when it misses. Removing taste uncertainty lifts conversion and order value at the same time.
The problem, in your own reviews

Taste is a blind gamble

14% of complaints are about taste, and buyers ask for the information outright. A box of 12 unknown wines is a leap of faith, so price and discount become the only decision cues.

"Vinerna smakar som saft" (the wines taste like juice)
"Jag saknar en beskrivning av respektive vin och vad det passar till." (I miss a description of each wine and what it pairs with)
The fix: a taste profile on every wine and box

The Trust Equation
In Swedish online alcohol, two levers decide the first purchase: is it legal, and will it arrive. dittvin is silent on the first and unreliable on the second.
Market Context · Highest Leverage Gap
Nobody Told the Swede It Is Legal
Sweden runs the Systembolaget monopoly. dittvin lawfully ships as a Danish cross border private importer (Vinhuset ApS). It never explains this, so doubt fills the gap.
What dittvin does today

Silence on legality

Zero mention of Systembolaget, private import, Swedish alcohol tax, or that VAT is included. The Danish operator identity is buried in the footer. The genuine assets (a 4.7 Trustpilot, award medals, Løvens Hule TV backing) sit far from the point of doubt.

"Så in i norden dåligt. leveransen har inte kommit fast det har gått 2 veckor. Tror detta r bedrägeri. VARNING" (I think this is fraud. WARNING)
What the best rival does

KRAN states it plainly

"Vi lyder under den svenska alkohollagstiftningen och regleras av svenska Skatteverket som distansförsäljare." Winefamly reassures with "Alla priser inkl. svensk alkoholskatt & moms." Winefinder, Winefamly and Supervin also show the Trygg E-handel trust mark. dittvin shows none of it.

The fix: a plain "Is this legal? Yes, and here is how" block on the PDP and cart, with the age check, the Danish import model, and tax included stated up front.

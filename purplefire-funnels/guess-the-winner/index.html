A guess the winner game in place of the video

A founder argues with a video and believes their own wrong answers. The game turns "I know my store" into "I guessed 4 of 10", which is the belief a done for you testing offer needs.

What changes

Today

Founders read finished results and a video asks them to believe them; ai-cro v1 gives a free audit to anyone.

New

The ad is round one: two versions of a real page, "Which one won?". The page is ten real A/B tests, sides shuffled, losing tests mixed in, the result after each guess. Round eleven is their store: URL and email bring five tests within 48 hours as a Loom and one still.

The test

Who it is for
Founders who never tested, founders with an agency, a head of ecommerce who shows the boss.
Hypothesis
Founders who play at least five rounds give a qualified store URL at a higher rate per dollar than the same audience sent to ai-cro v1, because a low score shows them they cannot pick winners by eye; if fewer than 40 percent of arm A visitors reach round three, the hypothesis is false. Five rounds played is counted by the event f04_round5_reached.
Arms
One cold audience split by Meta's A/B test tool. A: the game. B: ai-cro v1 untouched (Daniel's OK).
Kill
Under 40 percent of arm A reach round three after 300 visitors.
Keep
At least 40 percent of arm A visitors reach round three; at least 8 percent of players who finish round ten give a qualified URL; more qualified URLs per dollar than arm B; at least 2 calls by Fri 29 Jan. Directional.
Budget
$80 a day for 14 days, about $1,120 Est. Build 22 to 32 hours Est.; 1 to 1.75 hours per qualified lead Est.

Shared with the other kits

Kit 03 arm B's five tests format; the January calendar; kit 02's slot board and Meta account, never on one audience at once.

Risks

  • Students may play (the revenue tap filters).
  • If the change always wins, players learn the pattern.
  • Current clients appear only anonymised, with screens redrawn; any brand Daniel excludes stays out of the rounds.

How to read it

Read on Fri 29 Jan, from three places:

  1. Ads Manager, A/B test report: spend and landing page views per arm.
  2. His tracker (the events his developer wires): arm A visitors who reach round three, reach round five, finish round ten, and send a store URL.
  3. GHL: qualified URLs per arm (tag f04_qualified for arm A; the store URL and revenue answer from ai-cro v1's quiz for arm B, judged against the same floor) and calls booked by Fri 29 Jan, by source tag.

The numbers: round three rate (kill and keep line one); round five rate (the hypothesis's "at least five rounds"); qualified URLs as a share of players who finish round ten (keep line two); qualified URLs per $100 spent, arm A against arm B (keep line three); calls booked (keep line four). At about $560 a side the read is directional by design: it shows which way to lean, not a proven winner. His own VSL ads bid for similar founders on the same dates, and the read says so.

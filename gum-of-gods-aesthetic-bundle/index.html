The problem

Modern life made us mouth breathers.

Nasal breathing produces nitric oxide, which dilates blood vessels and increases oxygen uptake by about 25% compared to mouth breathing. Mouth breathing dries the airway, lowers oxygen saturation, and disrupts the sleep cycle. Over 70 million Americans live with chronic breathing issues, most rooted in airway posture, not allergies.

The problem

Soft food killed our jaw development.

Ancestral diets required hours of chewing per day. Modern diets need almost none. Without daily mechanical resistance, the masseter shrinks, the jawline softens, and the upper airway narrows. The fix is not aesthetic. It is structural retraining.

How long until I see results?

Most chewers feel a tighter jaw and clearer breathing inside two weeks. Visible jawline change in photos usually shows up between week four and week eight, depending on body fat and how often you chew. The 30 day window is enough to know if the ritual is for you.

Is mastic gum safe for TMJ or jaw issues?

Mastic is gentler on the joint than hard synthetic gums because it warms and softens as you chew. If you have an active TMJ flare, start with one piece for ten minutes a day and build from there. If you have a diagnosed disorder, check with your dentist or physician first.

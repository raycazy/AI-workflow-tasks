Rules for the copy

  • Locked, do not rewrite: the hero headline and sub (Dylan's ad lines, verbatim), the button text Claim Your Glass, prices, dates, the approved claims. Everything else on this page is example copy: rewrite it within the budget.
  • One message on every section: $200 per standard size card until Jan 31, $600 at launch, save $400; graded blind on the day you choose; lost or damaged, replaced or paid out.
  • Every section answers one objection from the list. If a section answers none, cut it.
  • Headers alone must carry the argument. Most visitors read only headers and buttons. H2s: 6 words.
  • One button text everywhere: Claim Your Glass. The microcopy under it changes to answer the fear at that point. Never write guarantee; state the promise: replaced or paid out.
  • Glass first. Poly appears once, as a text link in the offer block.
  • Real urgency only: the Jan 31 deadline and the $600 launch price. No timers, no stock counters.
  • Approved Glass claims: aerospace glass in a metal frame (the client's own phrase), resists scratches, seals like a vacuum, fits every card size, stacks with magnets, the only grader in glass. Not allowed: UV protection, resale value, made in USA, any other grader by name, up to 80% off (true for Poly only). Impact resistance is unconfirmed until the product team signs it off.
  • Confirmed in HGA's content pack and safe to state: graded blind to a published standard, then audited; four subgrades and the Grade Report; the chip in the label (RFID: write scan, never tap, never NFC or NFT); lost or damaged in our care, replaced or paid out; the grading calendar opens Jan 18 and grading starts Feb 1.
  • Pricing scope: $200 is per standard size card. Booklets, folds and decks are booked separately at $10 per fold. Cards over $10,000 carry a 2% insurance and handling charge, paid at booking. Say it in the FAQ at least.
  • Call what they buy a pass. Never write the housing name and pass together. No guaranteed turnaround, clock, due date, 15 business days or $1 a day credit. Never promise when the card comes back; the buyer picks the grading day.
  • Reviews: only real collectors, first name plus last initial, before and after, no grader names. Until then the slots stay empty.
  • House style: no em dashes, no en dashes, no hyphenated compound modifiers, no strings of three or more fragments. Write UCollect with a capital U and C on this site. Never: patented, Club U, custom labels, glass box, black box, marketplace.
  • Links out: the product page and the privacy policy. No links to the website.

Notes

Why the current pages are too long

  • Eleven sections against eight here. The booklet diagram, the six card category row, the five step demo and the calendar each repeat a point another section already made, so they flatten the value built before them.
  • The hero carries five or six checkmarks and a product led headline. A visitor cannot name the offer in two seconds.
  • The price first appears in section nine. Here it is in the promo bar, the hero, the offer block and the final CTA.
  • The same button text repeats seven times with nothing under it. Here the button stays the same and the microcopy under it changes to answer the fear at that point.
  • No section answers the first objection (will HGA still exist?) or states the risk reversal.

Open items before any of this goes live

  • Impact resistance and cracking: unconfirmed. No line about it until the product team signs it off.
  • The Oct 21 notes record a 30 day turnaround line from Trevor; HGA's later content pack removed every turnaround promise. The page follows the pack. Confirm which stands.
  • The payout basis for "replaced or paid out" (declared value?) is not stated anywhere.
  • Price scope: $200 per standard size card, $10 per fold for booklets, 2% over $10,000. Confirm these are current.
  • Reviews: none exist yet. The three benefits and the proof strip each have room for one real collector quote.

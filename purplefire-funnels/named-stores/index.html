What changes

Today

Every funnel asks for a call first, and the only thing a founder gets before it is advice (the ai-cro v1 audit, the VSL's five tests). Nobody receives finished work on their own page.

New

We pick 16 stores by hand. Daniel sends each founder or head of ecommerce one short message about their own product page: a Loom of about 40 seconds and one still. Nothing is hosted.

The test

Who it is for
Founders in a code freeze, founders who have never run an A/B test, and a head of ecommerce who needs something to forward.
Hypothesis
Founders shown one finished change on a copy of their own product page reply and book calls where founders shown the five tests we would run first do not.
Shares
The five tests workflow with kits 02 and 04; kit 02's calendars and slot board; the vault cards; the GHL follow up template.

Keep, kill and cost

Keep
2 or more qualified calls booked across both arms by the read date, and 3 or more positive replies from arm A.
Kill
No positive reply from arm A's 8 by Thu 10 Dec on the December path, or by Tue 12 Jan on the January path.
Cost
40 to 66 of our hours. Media $0; Store Leads optional, about $250. Est.

Risks

  • It can feel presumptuous (kind tone, video deleted on request).
  • A change that breaks on a phone costs trust.
  • Our hours per store have no source, and 8 a side is directional.
  • A hosted copy waits for a keep and a legal read.

How we read it

Numbers
Positive replies per arm and booked 15 minute planning calls per arm. A positive reply is "January", a question, or a calendar tap from a store at or above $150k a month. The counted event is a qualified call: a booked 15 minute planning call with a founder or head of ecommerce at a Shopify store doing $150k a month or more.
Where they come from
The tracking sheet, one row per store, with GHL tags as the backup.
When
The kill read on Thu 10 Dec (or Tue 12 Jan), the full read on Fri 18 Dec (or Fri 29 Jan).
How far it goes
At 8 stores an arm the read is directional. Three replies against one suggests a difference and proves none. A keep earns a second, larger list the next month.

Picking and splitting the stores

How the 16 stores are picked

A store goes on the sheet only if all of these are true:

  1. It runs on Shopify, checked from the storefront.
  2. It is likely at or above $150k a month (Est., from Store Leads or traffic signals). This estimate is never stated to the store as fact.
  3. It runs Meta ads now, checked in the Meta Ad Library on the day of the pick.
  4. Its category has a vault winner.
  5. Its product page visibly lacks one of the five patterns.
  6. A founder or head of ecommerce is reachable on a channel the legal read allows.
  7. It is in a country the legal read clears.

Never on the sheet: current clients, his pipeline, past clients (they belong to kit 01), and anyone on his do not contact list.

Count: 16 stores for this run, plus a few spares used only to replace a store whose copy breaks or whose page changes before sending. A spare takes the arm of the store it replaces.

Pattern before the split: fill and lock the best fit pattern for all 16 stores before the coin is flipped; arm A builds that pattern, and arm B names it as test one with the same proof line.

How the 16 are split into arms

  1. Sort the 16 by best fit pattern, then by revenue estimate.
  2. Pair them in that order: stores 1 and 2, 3 and 4, and so on, for 8 pairs.
  3. Flip a coin for each pair. Heads: the first store goes to arm A, the second to arm B. Tails: the other way round.
  4. Write the arm on the sheet and lock it before anything is built. Nobody moves a store between arms after this, for any reason.

Pairing keeps the patterns and store sizes even across the arms at 8 a side, and the coin keeps the choice out of our hands.

Send days: the odd pairs (1, 3, 5, 7) go on the first send day and the even pairs (2, 4, 6, 8) on the second, so each day carries 4 arm A and 4 arm B messages and the patterns and store sizes spread over both days.

If that week has no seat left (2 seats a week), hold the next week that has one and name it in the first line.

Follow up after four working days

One follow up only, sent in the same thread or chat as the first message, to rows with no reply and no opt out. Use the viewed version if the Loom shows a view from anyone but us, the not viewed version otherwise. After this, nobody writes again unless they reply.

Nothing goes to Danish prospects until Daniel names his countries and has a legal read.

Not viewed, both arms: email, the still goes in the body, not as an attachment; LinkedIn, attach the still again.

Reply for a store under the floor

Use when a reply or the calendar booking shows the store is under $150k a month. It is not counted as a lead, and no call is booked.

The consent rule (a step nobody can skip)

Before any first message or follow up goes to a store, its row on the pick sheet must show Legal: cleared, with the date and the country. A row without that date is not sent, by anyone, for any reason.

Nothing is sent to anyone in Denmark until Daniel has named his countries and a legal read has confirmed this format. Danish marketing law (section 10(1) of the marketing act) bans approaching anyone by electronic mail for direct marketing without prior consent, and the Consumer Ombudsman treats LinkedIn Conversation Ads as electronic mail (Dansk Erhverv).

Also for the legal read, all unverified today: whether the Danish ban covers business recipients and LinkedIn messages and invitations; whether Germany needs prior consent for business email; the opt out rules in the UK and the US; and whether a founder whose details came from Store Leads or a contact finder must be told the source at first contact (if yes, a source line is added to every first message).

Every message carries the opt out line. An opt out is marked on the row the same day and the Loom is deleted.
